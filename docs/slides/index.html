Friends, Not the Algorithm

Replace my YouTube recommendation feed with my circle: the people who know me, and the creators I watch.

Bowei · working title · core-loop mock screens
← → to move   A all screens on one page   F full screen
The problem

YouTube decides what I watch next.

And it decides on what keeps me watching, not on who I am.

1
The feed picks for watch time
Not for me. My history tunes it, and leaving means starting cold.
2
My people would pick better
My wife and coworkers know me. But they don't know exactly what I'm into, and there's no good way to say "you'd love this, here's why".
3
Creators never hear from me
The niche channels I watch (around 10k subscribers) get a view count and maybe a public comment. They make things for me and never know me.
The idea

Show what I'm into by reacting. Let my circle pick for me.

1
I react
Video, voice, or text, pinned to the exact moment that grabbed me.
2
My circle sees it
Inside their own YouTube, through a browser extension. Nothing is public.
3
They send picks
From their own feeds, each with their reaction and why I'd like it.
4
Picks are my feed
I react to their picks, they learn me better, the next picks get better.
"Manifestation": show your dreams to your circle, and they help you reach them.The coworker's post this borrows from
The demo, screen by screen

Nine screens, one loop.

The demo loop, one screen per interaction. Bowei reacts and sends; his wife watches his reactions inside her YouTube and sends a pick back; it lands in Bowei's YouTube. Sign-in and invites are left out: everyone in these mocks already has the extension. Every interaction is written out in the interaction notes (link at the bottom right).

Why this dismantles network effects

YouTube holds me with three locks. This loosens all three.

Creators and viewers
They stay on YouTube for each other. The videos stay there, but creators and viewers get a direct line YouTube doesn't sit in.
Recommendations
The feed improves with my history, so leaving means starting cold. Picks from people who know me replace it.
The social layer
Today it's a link in a chat or a public comment. Reactions move into something I own, attached to the video itself.

The honest limit: the videos still live on YouTube. What leaves is the part that makes it sticky: what I watch next, who I talk to about it, and my relationship with the people who made it.
